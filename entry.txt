World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Iran said it is still waiting for a US answer to its seven conditions for peace talks, and its security chief Mohsen Rezaei said the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until they are met; three tankers caught fire after hitting mines there, and Brent crude rose above $92 a barrel. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that economic pressure on Iran was working well enough that no major combat operations were needed. Ukraine and Russia agreed a large prisoner exchange as US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive and quite substantive". The US House voted 211-204 to send an impeachment resolution against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it, and Republicans chose Jim Jordan as their next House leader, beating Steve Scalise 106-73. At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, where 102 leaders gathered, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned that slow action had put the world "on a path to destruction"; the United States stayed away for a second year. Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said. Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-deadliest outbreak on record. The US Justice Department charged three alleged Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in the US and Europe. NATO jets scrambled after drones strayed into Baltic airspace, and British police arrested a US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia. Israel swore in a new parliament without a government. Former Malaysian prime minister Najib Razak left prison for house arrest after four years over the 1MDB scandal. A fog-related motorway pile-up in Bulgaria killed 12 people, and a mass shooting in Krakow killed six and injured 15. (Sources: Reuters, BBC, NYT, Al Jazeera, AP.)
